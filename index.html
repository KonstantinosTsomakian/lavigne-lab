Gene Regulation & Epigenetics · IMBB-FORTH

Transcription, genome integrity and cell fate

The Lavigne Lab studies the molecular mechanisms that govern gene expression programmes in health and disease, from RNA polymerase II dynamics and genome maintenance under stress to the regulatory networks that shape cell fate. We combine experimental biochemistry, next-generation sequencing and computational modelling.

Publications Lab members

Research

Our work is organised around three connected research areas.

Transcription dynamics

Initiation, promoter-proximal pausing and elongation of RNA polymerase II at protein-coding and non-coding genes, including lncRNAs and enhancer RNAs.

Genome integrity under stress

How cells maintain the integrity of their genomic DNA under environmental and genetic stress, and how transcription and DNA repair are coordinated.

Gene regulatory networks and cell fate

How transcriptional and chromatin regulatory networks shape differentiation trajectories during development and in pathogenic settings.

Approach

We integrate wet-lab and computational approaches within a single research programme.

Experimental systems

Human cell lines and mouse models, studied with time- and space-resolved (4D) biochemistry

Genomics

Bulk and single-cell sequencing of chromatin, transcription and 3D genome organisation

Computation

Bioinformatics, simulations and machine learning on in-house and public data

Insight

Principles of transcriptional and epigenetic regulation, and how they fail in disease
